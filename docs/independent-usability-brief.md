# Independent laptop-workspace walkthrough

Use a participant who has not helped design the app. The owner has offered to arrange this session; no participant result has been collected yet.

Open the account-free fictional example at http://127.0.0.1:5173/. The local servers must be running on the participant's computer, or the participant can use the owner's computer. A localhost link does not make the app remotely accessible. Do not enter real finances, create credentials, or save/delete account data.

## Give these tasks to the participant

1. Explore Alex's example. Explain what Alex needs and what you would do next about the current laptop.
2. Review one fictional replacement laptop and try comparing it with the current laptop. Describe what the result means and any information you still need.
3. Explore when Alex could afford a replacement. Change the monthly contribution and explain what changed. If useful, explore a spending-change scenario.
4. Explain what would happen if you closed this tab now. Describe how you think saving a decision and keeping a historical review would work.

Ask the participant to say what they expect before activating a control. If wording or navigation is confusing, ask what they expected instead. Do not explain the intended answer before they try. If they need help, provide it and record that assistance.

Evaluate understanding of draft versus confirmed data, unknown values, eligibility versus affordability, original versus alternative savings plans, and explicit saving. Observe whether those distinctions are clear; do not treat agreement with the app's recommendation as the success criterion. The participant remains free to choose whether to replace a laptop.

Share observations without passwords, real budgets or private account screenshots. One walkthrough is useful feedback, not proof that every usability or accessibility issue has been found.
