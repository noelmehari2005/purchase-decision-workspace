# Product brief and journey

## Problem and value

Choosing a laptop involves suitability, uncertainty, cost, and timing. A spreadsheet or specification list may describe products without connecting them to the user's actual work or a feasible purchase plan.

This workspace connects needs, evidence, product comparison, and conditional savings scenarios. It should also support the outcome "keep what I own" or "investigate and save." A recommendation never commits the user to purchasing or changing spending.

## First-release boundary

- United States, USD, laptops only.
- Initial persona: fictional Alex, a CS student preparing for more demanding programming. The journey also serves programmers outside school.
- General programming, web development, local backend/database use, occasional Docker, IDEs, and concurrent development tools.
- Guided task checklist, custom needs, current/upcoming needs, and relevant dates.
- Manual assessment and evidence; no device scanning, diagnosis, automatic product discovery, or universal hardware rules.
- Icecat product links and exact manufacturer identifiers are initial import inputs. Unsupported links are retained for manual entry. Manufacturer code lookup must also identify the brand and resolve ambiguity.
- No bank integration, financing model, AI product adviser, or automatic spending cuts.

The larger electronics/appliances concept is preserved, but this release does not implement appliances. Vehicles precede housing in future category expansion. Do not design those category models now.

## Alex's journey

1. **Needs:** Alex selects tasks, optional required tools, current/upcoming needs, and deadlines. Course numbers do not determine urgency.
2. **Current laptop:** Record known specifications and whether tasks work adequately, struggle, cannot run, or are unknown. Give optional plain-language help finding information.
3. **Candidate entry:** Paste an Icecat link or enter product identity. Import into a draft; review the exact configuration, correct values, and retain unknowns. Manual entry is always available.
4. **Comparison:** Keep the current device as a baseline. Group replacements by mandatory eligibility, then explain preference tradeoffs. Show purchase timing separately.
5. **Plan:** Enter purchase savings and a chosen contribution. Optionally use income, expense categories, other commitments, a monthly buffer, and a deadline to explore contributions.
6. **Scenario:** Select a possible expense reduction and how much of it goes toward the purchase. Compare against the unchanged original plan.
7. **Decision:** Save reasoning, unresolved questions, candidate revisions, and assumptions. Later revisions must not rewrite earlier snapshots silently.

## Illustrative arithmetic, not financial recommendations

Alex has $300 in purchase savings and chooses $150/month. An $800 estimated total needs four contributions; a $1,100 total needs six. An optional $40/month reduction entirely allocated to the purchase changes the contribution to $190 and those counts to three and five.

A $900 hard maximum excludes the $1,100 option regardless of eventual savings. Removing the ceiling removes that exclusion, while the longer timeline remains visible. A timeline is conditional on unchanged inputs, not a finding of affordability.

Alex's 16 GB RAM / 512 GB storage requirements in the wireframes are fictional explicit choices, not universal recommendations for programmers. Housing examples from earlier brainstorming were also fictional, not user financial information.

## Success evidence

- A user can complete a decision with incomplete technical knowledge.
- A user can explain why each candidate is eligible, failed, or unconfirmed.
- An import reduces typing while preserving configuration correctness; a name-only import does not establish the desired usability benefit.
- A user can distinguish suitability, preference fit, and purchase timing.
- Scenario changes preserve the original budget; saved reasoning is revisable and inspectable.
- The student owner can explain requirements, design boundaries, calculations, and verification, including AI assistance.

No adoption, usability, extraction-accuracy, or professional-review claims have been established. These are outcomes to evaluate.
